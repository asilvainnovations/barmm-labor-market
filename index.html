CORE POLICY SIGNAL

High employment coexists with rising underemployment and a large share of own-account and unpaid family work. Workforce policy must therefore move beyond job counts towards productive employment, adequate hours and earnings, enterprise capability and stronger transitions into investment-linked occupations.

What this tells us

Participation was unchanged, while unemployment and underemployment increased and the employment rate eased. The policy signal therefore includes job adequacy and access to sufficient hours, not only job counts.

What this tells us

Agriculture, forestry and fishing remains the largest employment base, closely followed by services. Industry accounts for a much smaller share. This is an employment distribution, not a measure of productivity or investment demand.

GRDP and Employment Structure

i

2024 GRDP share compared with 2025 employment share; descriptive comparison only

What this tells us

Agriculture has a larger share of employment than of GRDP, while industry has a larger share of GRDP than of employment. Different reference years and denominators mean this is descriptive—not a productivity ratio.

Annual Employment Change

i

Change in employed persons by subsector, thousands

What this tells us

Gains in accommodation and food services and construction occurred alongside a substantial decline in agriculture and forestry. These shifts warrant investigation of worker transitions and employer demand; they do not establish occupational shortages.

Source: PSA BARMM 2025 Labour Force Survey regional release and PSA BARMM 2024 Regional Accounts. Reference years and denominators differ for GRDP and employment shares; this is not a productivity ratio.

Workforce Development Implications

Expand enterprise-linked and community-based delivery; align training with project-specific occupational requirements; track placement in related occupations, retention, wages and employer satisfaction; and address access barriers across geography and gender.
